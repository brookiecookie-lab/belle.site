The problem

A real citation can still fail.

The paper can exist. The journal can be reputable. The DOI can resolve. And the claim attached to it can still be scientifically vulnerable.

Claim ↔ Source MismatchThe paper is real, but it does not support what the report says it supports.
Causal OverstatementAssociation, mechanism, or population-level evidence is expressed as stronger causation than the evidence permits.
Applicability FailurePopulation, dose, exposure route, duration, endpoint, or circumstances do not adequately match the proposition.
Missing Contrary EvidenceThe cited literature supports the argument, but a serious adversarial search reveals material evidence pointing the other way.
What gets tested

Claim → evidence → inference → conclusion.

Scientific red-teaming operates at the exact point where ordinary proofreading, citation management, and bibliography checks stop.

Source integrityDoes the study exist and what is its publication status?
Claim-to-source fidelityDoes the source support the exact proposition?
Study designWhat can the design actually establish?
Statistics and uncertaintyAre effect size, intervals, denominators, and absolute risk represented fairly?
Causal inferenceCould confounding, selection, measurement error, reverse causation, or adjustment choices explain the result?
Population and exposure fitDoes the evidence actually apply to the circumstances at issue?
Mechanistic evidenceIs biological plausibility being asked to prove more than it can?
Contrary evidenceWhat would a technically competent opposing reviewer find?
Corrections and retractionsHas the scientific status of the source changed?
OverstatementIs the wording more certain than the evidence?
Sample ruling

What you are actually buying.

Not a generic literature memo. A claim-by-claim vulnerability analysis designed to expose the scientific attack surface.

Claim

“Exposure X increases the risk of Disease Y by approximately 300%.”

Ruling
OVERSTATED
Why

The cited retrospective observational study reports an adjusted odds ratio of approximately 3.1 with substantial uncertainty. It establishes an observed association in its study population; it does not independently establish causation or transfer automatically to materially different exposure circumstances.

Attack surface
Observational designResidual confoundingImprecise estimatePopulation applicabilityOdds ratio framed as risk increaseCausal language exceeds evidence
More defensible
“In the studied population, Exposure X was associated with higher odds of Disease Y, although the estimate was imprecise and does not independently establish causation.”
