# THE PAGES WE KEEP × THE JOURNAL
## MONTHLY EDITORIAL EDITION TEMPLATE 001

**Canonical use:** Fashion/editorial monthly reading product  
**Cadence:** 2 featured books + 1 evergreen per month  
**Target length:** 20–40 designed pages per featured treatment  
**Doctrine:** SAME BRAIN. DIFFERENT DEPTH. DIFFERENT CUSTOMER.

---

# COVER

BOOK TITLE  
AUTHOR / SUBJECT  
MONTH · FEATURE NUMBER  
One-line editorial thesis

Visual direction:
- Book-cover identification
- One strong rights-cleared/publicity/archive image where lawful
- World/archetype routing
- Edition status / publication data

---

# 01 · WHY THIS BOOK NOW

Answer in 1–2 pages:

- What is happening around this book now?
- Why does it belong in this publication?
- What demand/cultural signal supports attention now?
- What makes our treatment different from an ordinary review?
- What will the reader understand by the end that a summary would not give her?

Required distinction:
**CURRENT SIGNAL / EDITORIAL FIT / COMMERCIAL FIT / CULTURAL FIT** stay separate.

---

# 02 · ENTRY: WHAT YOU NEED BEFORE YOU READ

Put necessary context on the page.

Possible modules:
- Who is this woman / author / subject?
- What does the public already think it knows?
- What chronology matters?
- What industry, era, law, technology, labor system, media environment, or cultural norm is pressing against the story?
- Which terms need defining?
- Which common assumptions need checking before we begin?

Rule:

> DON'T JUST ASK THE READER TO FIND THE CONTEXT. PUT THE EVIDENCE ON THE PAGE.

---

# 03 · THE PUBLIC STORY

Build the narrative the audience was given.

May include:
- headlines
- interviews
- publicity
- paparazzi / red-carpet framing
- magazine profiles
- television appearances
- social media
- brand campaigns
- adaptation publicity
- scandals
- repeated cultural shorthand

Separate:
FACT / SELF-REPORT / PRESS FRAME / PUBLIC MYTH / EDITORIAL INFERENCE.

---

# 04 · THE BOOK'S STORY

What does the book itself claim, reveal, revise, complicate, or refuse?

Use short quotations only when legally appropriate.

Track:
- major claims
- chronology
- self-description
- omissions / silences where analytically relevant
- moments of changed interpretation
- places where the memoir/biography/fiction challenges the public story

Memoir rule:
**A memoir claim is evidence of what the memoirist reports and remembers. It is not automatically independent proof of every underlying event.**

---

# 05 · SELECTED PAGES WE KEEP LENSES

Choose only 1–3 primary lenses per book.

Possible recurring lenses:

### IMAGE / PUBLIC PERSONA
How fashion, beauty, photography, styling, publicity, branding, and performance construct the woman the public thinks it knows.

### STYLE AS IDENTITY
What clothing, beauty, costume, objects, or aesthetic repetition communicates before anyone speaks.

### WHO TOLD THE STORY?
Memoir, press, interview, publicist, partner, family, studio, publisher, fandom, critic, court, archive.

### AMAZING HOW THEY LOVE YOU WHEN YOU'RE GONE
Use only where evidence shows a meaningful before/after shift in valuation, framing, sympathy, commercial use, collectibility, or narrative control.

### THE REVOLUTION OUTSIDE / THE REVOLUTION INSIDE
What political, legal, social, economic, technological, or cultural change is pressing against the intimate story?

### MYTH VS. RECORD
What has become “known” through repetition, and what does the documented record actually support?

### WHO BENEFITS FROM THE DOMINANT STORY?
Follow money, status, institutional power, publicity, brand value, or narrative convenience.

### WOMEN'S LABOR
Paid, unpaid, emotional, domestic, reproductive, creative, image, caregiving, promotional, or invisible labor.

### ADAPTATION IS AN ARGUMENT
What changed when the story crossed into film, television, stage, documentary, or another medium?

---

# 06 · EVIDENCE ON THE PAGE

A compact evidence dossier, not footnote theater.

For each major claim:
- CLAIM
- SOURCE TYPE
- SOURCE
- DATE
- WHAT IT PROVES
- WHAT IT DOES NOT PROVE
- STATUS: VERIFIED / SELF-REPORT / CONTESTED / INFERENCE / OPEN

Prioritize:
1. primary/contemporaneous evidence
2. authoritative direct sources
3. high-quality secondary reporting
4. criticism/analysis
5. community reaction only when the community reaction itself is the subject

---

# 07 · FASHION / BEAUTY / COSTUME / IMAGE LANE

Only when relevant.

Possible modules:
- signature silhouette
- public styling eras
- red-carpet evolution
- costume as character
- beauty contract / brand ambassadorship
- hair / makeup / fragrance / body discourse
- designers, stylists, makers
- archive objects
- image rights / publicity
- what was mocked then vs. canonized later
- shoppable or affiliate objects only when lawful, accurate, useful, and disclosed

Do not force commerce into trauma or grief.

---

# 08 · MEDIA / PRESS / SCANDAL LANE

Build comparison panels:

**THEN / LATER**  
**HEADLINE / RECORD**  
**WHAT SHE SAID / WHAT OTHERS SAID**  
**FIRST REPORT / RETROSPECTIVE**  
**PUBLICITY / DOCUMENTATION**

Ask:
- Which phrases repeated?
- What became shorthand?
- What facts were foregrounded or buried?
- What changed later?
- Was the later treatment more generous?
- If so, why?

---

# 09 · ADAPTATION / SCREEN / INTERVIEW

When applicable:
- rights status
- adaptation status
- casting
- costume
- music
- setting
- compression / omission
- changed point of view
- interview circuits
- documentary framing

Never imply an adaptation exists until verified.

---

# 10 · COUNTERREADING

A Pages We Keep object must contain friction.

Include at least one:
- alternative interpretation
- contrary evidence
- limitation of the book's perspective
- criticism
- uncertainty
- unresolved chronology
- distinction between emotional truth and independently verifiable fact

This is not “both sides” for its own sake.
It is anti-flattening.

---

# 11 · THE RABBIT HOLES

> WE DON'T ASSIGN THE RABBIT HOLE. WE BUILD THE RABBIT HOLE.

Provide 3–7 curated outward trails.

Each trail gets:
- why follow it
- 2–5 starting sources/objects
- what question it can answer
- what new lane it opens

Rabbit holes may include:
people · archives · interviews · court records · photographs · places · designers · films · beauty campaigns · memoirs · social history · music · objects · law · labor · fandom · adaptations.

---

# 12 · SOURCE TRAILS

A short, useful source map:
- START HERE
- PRIMARY / CONTEMPORANEOUS
- DEEPER CONTEXT
- CRITICISM / COUNTERREADING
- VISUAL / ARCHIVE
- FOLLOW NEXT

Do not dump an undifferentiated bibliography.

---

# 13 · WHAT TO NOTICE

One designed page of reader prompts.

Not homework.
Not comprehension questions.

Examples:
- Notice who gets quoted and who gets summarized.
- Notice when clothing appears as self-authorship versus costume imposed by others.
- Notice where chronology gets fuzzy.
- Notice what the press treats as character evidence.
- Notice what becomes sympathetic only in hindsight.

---

# 14 · WHAT TO FOLLOW NEXT

Bridge to:
- another Journal story
- a World/archetype
- a related book
- an adaptation
- a deeper standalone The Pages We Keep study when one exists
- a fashion/beauty lane
- a person or cultural sequence worth following

The monthly object must still feel complete without purchasing the deeper product.

---

# 15 · PROVENANCE / RIGHTS / EDITION NOTE

Record:
- exact book edition used
- publication metadata
- source freeze date
- rights state of visual assets
- substantive artifact SHA-256
- revision/version
- known open questions

---

# LENGTH MODEL

Suggested allocation for a 28–32 page feature:

- Cover / contents: 2
- Why now + entry context: 3
- Public story + book story: 4
- Primary lenses: 6
- Evidence dossier: 4
- fashion/beauty/media/adaptation: 4
- counterreading: 2
- rabbit holes: 3
- source trails / what to notice / next: 3
- provenance: 1

Modules expand or contract by title.

---

# QUALITY GATE

A monthly treatment is not ready unless it can answer:

1. Why this book now?
2. Why us?
3. What context did we put on the page?
4. Which claims have evidence?
5. What remains self-report, disputed, or open?
6. Which 1–3 recurring lenses materially sharpen the book?
7. What visual lane earns its space?
8. Which rabbit holes did we actually build?
9. Does the reader leave understanding more—not merely knowing the plot?
10. Does this stand on its own without cheapening the deeper Pages We Keep product?
