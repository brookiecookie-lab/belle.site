## Editorial thesis

*Yesteryear* gives us an unusually clean way to do what The Pages We Keep is supposed to do: put the world pressing against the story on the page.

A modern tradwife influencer sells a beautiful version of “traditional” womanhood and then confronts the material conditions of 1855. Our edition should not merely discuss the satire. It should build the evidence underneath its central tension:

**THE AESTHETIC OF TRADITION vs. THE LABOR OF TRADITION**

# 08 · COUNTERREADING
## THE PROBLEM IS NOT THAT WOMEN LIKE HOME

Avoid a simplistic rebuttal to domestic life.

Complications:
- domestic labor can be meaningful
- homemaking can be chosen
- modern paid work can also exploit women
- “lean in” feminism can flatten caregiving desires
- paid work does not guarantee autonomy
- nostalgia can identify a real modern dissatisfaction even when its historical solution is false

Core question:
**What legitimate hunger is the fantasy answering?**

This is more interesting than “tradwives bad.”

# 12 · WHAT TO NOTICE

- Notice every task that looks effortless.
- Notice who is absent from the frame.
- Notice where “natural” requires expensive modern infrastructure.
- Notice how clothing changes when it must be maintained rather than merely photographed.
- Notice whether the satire criticizes a woman, an ideology, an economy, or all three.
- Notice which modern freedoms Natalie only recognizes after losing them.
- Notice what modern life fails to provide that makes the fantasy emotionally persuasive.
